# A continuous-time twin promises any bandwidth: a controller sampled every T has a hard cap ωnT < 2, two distinct failure modes, and a deadbeat point the twin cannot see

*Stylised: a unit mass under a sampled PD loop, pure Python, every number is from `experiments/results.txt` (deterministic, about a second). The "real" system is itself simulated; no lab or field data. Out-of-model and negative results are marked.*

## Question
A twin of a robot is usually simulated with the controller running in continuous time or at a much higher rate than the hardware. If gains are tuned in it for speed (high natural frequency `ωn`), how fast can the real controller, which samples every `T` and holds its command, be before the loop is unstable, what does it do on the way, and what does the twin get wrong?

## Model
Real: `ẍ = u`, `u(t) = −kp x(kT) − kd v(kT)` for `t ∈ [kT, (k+1)T)`. Exact discretisation in `(x, v)`: `x⁺ = x + Tv + T²u/2`, `v⁺ = v + Tu`, closed-loop trace `2 − kpT²/2 − kdT`, determinant `1 − kdT + kpT²/2`. Twin: the same law in continuous time, `s² + kd s + kp`, stable for every positive gain with decay rate `ζωn`. With `kp = ωn²`, `kd = 2ζωn` and time scaling, everything depends on `(ζ, ε = ωnT)`.

## Results
1. **Exact stability window.** The Jury conditions reduce to `det < 1` (`kd > kpT/2`), `1 + det − tr = kpT² > 0`, and `1 + det + tr = 4 − 2kdT > 0`, so the loop is stable iff `kpT/2 < kd < 2/T`, i.e. `ε < min(4ζ, 1/ζ)`. This agreed with the multiplier moduli in 48 of 48 (ζ ∈ {0.1 … 5}, ε/limit ∈ {0.5 … 2}) cases, and the exact discretisation agreed with an RK4 simulation of the continuous plant (command held over each period) to 1.8·10⁻¹⁴ over five cases and 40 samples. The twin is stable everywhere and cannot flag it.
2. **A cap for every damping.** Eliminating `kd` gives `kpT² < 4`: `ωn < 2/T = ωs/π`, i.e. `fn < fs/π ≈ 0.32 fs`, attained only at ζ = ½ (limits 0.40, 1.2, 2.0, 1.43, 1.0, 0.5, 0.2 for ζ = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 2, 5). Random search over 2·10⁵ log-uniform `(kp, kd)` at T = 1: 44 808 of 134 797 draws below the cap were stable, 0 of 65 203 above it.
3. **Two mechanisms, one of which the lag twin also sees.** For ζ < ½ the binding side is `kd = kpT/2`: determinant 1, a complex pair on the unit circle at angle `θ = 2 asin(ε/2)` per sample (0.4027, 0.8230, 1.2870, 1.8546 at ε = 0.4 … 1.6, equal to the closed form to nine digits), so the loop hunts at 1.007, 1.029, 1.072, 1.159 times the twin's own `ωn`. This is exactly the Routh limit of `lag-twin` (`τ* = kd/kp`) with `τ = T/2`: the hold behaves like a half-sample lag at the boundary (zeta = 0.3, ωn = 0.6: `τ* = 1`, sampled limit `T = 2`). For ζ > ½ the binding side is `kdT = 2`: a real multiplier at −1 (−1.000000 for ζ = 0.6 … 5), a period-2 chatter at the Nyquist rate, with the other multiplier `1 − ε²/2` inside the circle (0.5, 0.875, 0.98 at ζ = 1, 2, 5). Nothing in a lag-only model predicts this mode; it is a limit on derivative gain, not bandwidth.
4. **Validation on a step test misses the cliff.** At ζ = 0.7 the sampled response at the sample times differs from the twin's by RMS 0.0085 (ε = 0.05), 0.030 (0.3), 0.053 (0.8) over 40 steps; with `T` as the small parameter at ωn = 1 over 40 time units the RMS error is 0.053 ε (0.0530 at ε = 0.01, 0.0566 at ε = 0.5): first order in `T`, small, and no hint that the loop is unstable at ε = 1.4286. Overshoot stays 4.6–5.6% (twin 4.6%) up to ε = 1, then jumps to 15.2% at 1.2 and the spectral radius goes 0.200 (ε = 1.2) → 0.918 (1.4) → 0.975 (1.42).
5. **The twin errs in both directions, and the bandwidth is non-monotone.** At ζ = 0.7 the real per-step contraction is *below* the twin's `e^{−ζε}` at every ε tried up to 1.2 (ε = 0.3: 0.791 vs 0.811; ε = 1: 0.316 vs 0.497; minimum 0.200 at ε = 1.2 vs the twin's 0.432), then rises to 0.918 at ε = 1.4 and 1 at the limit. The deadbeat gains `kpT² = 1`, `kdT = 3/2` (ε = 1, ζ = ¾) give both multipliers 0 and `x = 0.5, 0, 0, 0` with no inter-sample overshoot (most negative `x(t)` 0.0), whereas the twin with the same gains has `x(T), x(2T), x(3T) = 0.702, 0.300, 0.067` and 2.8% overshoot. The direction of the error depends on damping: the minimum real radius over ε is 0.906, 0.707, 0.529, 0.200, 0, 0.455, 0.560, 0.796 for ζ = 0.3, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.75, 0.9, 1, 1.5 (at ε = 0.60, 1.00, 1.20, 1.20, 1.00, 0.89, 0.83, 0.61) against the twin's `e^{−ζε}` at the same ε of 0.835, 0.607, 0.487, 0.432, 0.472, 0.449, 0.437, 0.403. So the real loop beats the twin only for ζ near 0.7–0.75 and is worse for ζ ≤ 0.6 and ζ ≥ 0.9; the twin's promise keeps growing with ε while the real radius bottoms out, so a "faster is better" tuning in the twin is not reliable in either direction.
6. **Design rule.** With margin `m = ε/ε_limit`, `ωn ≤ m·min(4ζ, 1/ζ)/T`. At ζ = 0.7, T = 10 ms: m = 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1 gives ωn ≤ 142.9, 107.1, 71.4, 35.7, 14.3 rad/s with spectral radius 1, 0.272, 0.505, 0.751, 0.900. The asymptotic time to 10⁻⁶ (spectral radius alone, ignoring transients) is 0.106, 0.202, 0.482, 1.313 s for m = 0.75 … 0.1, against the twin's `ln(10⁶)/(ζωn)` = 0.184, 0.276, 0.553, 1.382 s: the sampled loop is slightly faster at every margin shown, so the margin costs speed only through the twin-blind cliff. A twin controller run at 1 kHz certifies ωn = 150 rad/s at ζ = 0.7; a 100 Hz real controller is unstable there (limit T = 9.52 ms) but stable at 142 rad/s (limit 10.06 ms).

## Limitations
Linear, scalar, exact PD on a double integrator, no noise, no computation delay (a one-sample compute delay moves the limits; that is `latency-twin`'s regime), no quantisation or saturation, and the comparison at sample times only (the hold's inter-sample behaviour enters only the deadbeat check). The "real" plant is the same family as the twin apart from the sampling, so model-form error is not tested. Only the window, the cap, the boundary multipliers and the deadbeat point are analytic; the best-ε values are from a 10⁻⁴-of-limit grid. Not evidence about any specific controller.

## Next steps
Sampling plus computation delay (a two-parameter window); sampled loops with a realistic discretised derivative filter; identifying `T_eff` (jitter, `sync-twin`) from logs; gain-scheduled bandwidth for multi-rate cascades; bandwidth caps for sampled attitude and manipulator loops against the twin's control rate.
