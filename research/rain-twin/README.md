# Rain twin: radar detection range under rain attenuation, why a twin run at the mean rain rate is the wrong twin, and what to run instead

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: monostatic radar, clear-air detection range `r0 = 20 km`, one-way rain attenuation `γ = 0.05 R` dB/km, rain present 10% of the time with exponential rate (mean 8 mm/h) when present; detection is deterministic given the rain rate: `4 ln(r0/r) ≥ 2κr`. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`): (1) the detection range is exactly `r_d(κ) = (2/κ) W(κ r0/2)` (Lambert W; residual of the fixed point ~1e-15), decreasing and convex in `κ`; (2) by Jensen the real mean range `E[r_d] = 19.32 km` exceeds the range at the mean rain rate, `r_d(κ(E R)) = 18.38 km` (gap 0.94 km; quadrature and 400k-draw Monte Carlo agree to 0.003 km), and the gap grows with attenuation (0.10 km at `k = 0.01` to 5.03 km at `k = 0.3`); (3) the detection probability at range `r` is exactly `1 − p·exp(−R*(r)/m)` with `R*(r)` the rain rate whose attenuation just defeats range `r`, floor `1−p = 0.90` for any `r < r0` (matches Monte Carlo to ≤0.0015); (4) quantiles commute with the monotone map but means do not: the median-rain twin reproduces the real median range exactly (14.586 km at `p = 0.8`) while the mean-rain twin gives 12.581 km; (5) against real outcomes over 2–20 km the mean-rain deterministic twin has Brier excess 0.0756 over the irreducible 0.0352, **worse than a clear-air twin (0.0026)** because rain is intermittent; a twin calibrated to match `E[r_d]` is 0.0330; the climatology ensemble with the true `(p, m)` has excess 0 and its misspecification cost is the exact `∫(Q−P)²` (0.0007 for `p_t = 0.05`, 0.0417 for `p_t = 0.5`).

cd research/rain-twin

**Builds on.** The radar sensor-simulation and sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>) and the weather/UAV digital-twin direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The radar equation and rain power-law attenuation are classical (Skolnik 2008; Marshall–Palmer; ITU-R P.838 form `γ = kR^α`, with `k`, `α` here chosen for illustration, not taken from the recommendation). Companion to `multipath-twin`, `radar-detection-twin` and `radar-clutter-twin`; scoring uses the Brier rule as in `twin-evaluation`.

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no radar or rain-gauge data; deterministic threshold detection, no noise or target fluctuation; exponential rain-rate distribution and a single rain rate along the whole path (no spatial rain cells or path-integrated variation); no rain backscatter clutter; single frequency parameterised only through `k`.
