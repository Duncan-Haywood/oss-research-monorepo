# Hiding synchronisation latency with stale outer steps: an exact stability limit, a no-free-lunch, and delay compensation

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
Overlapped ("streaming") DiLoCo-style training applies the outer step to a pseudo-gradient that is `τ` rounds old so that a synchronisation costing `C` inner-step equivalents runs behind compute. On a quadratic with per-mode outer curvature `s = 1−(1−ηa)^H ∈ (0,1]` (see `outer-momentum`), the stale outer step `x_{t+1} = x_t − α s x_{t−τ}` has characteristic polynomial `z^{τ+1} − z^τ + a`, `a = αs`, and we show: (i) it converges iff `a < 2 sin(π/(4τ+2)) ≈ π/(2τ)` (verified to 0.1% for `τ ≤ 32`), so plain averaging `α = 1` on saturated modes (`s = 1`) is marginal at `τ = 1` and diverges for `τ ≥ 2` (growth 1.15–1.18 per round); (ii) the fastest single mode has a double root and radius exactly `τ/(τ+1)`; (iii) **no free lunch**: with `τ+1` rounds in flight the period shrinks by `τ+1`, but the tuned step is capped so that `(τ+1)·2 sin(π/(4τ+2))` falls from 2 (`τ = 0, 1`) to `π/2`; at `κ = 1000, H = 16` overlap is 1.01×, 1.09×, 1.16×, 1.22×, 1.25× *slower* than blocking at `τ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16`, and an exhaustive `(H,τ)` search never picks `τ > 0`; (iv) if the server can extrapolate the stale gradient with a curvature estimate ratio `λ` (`x_{t+1} = x_t − α(g_{t−τ} + λ s (x_t − x_{t−τ}))`, roots `z^{τ+1} − (1−λa)z^τ + (1−λ)a`), `λ = 1` removes the delay exactly (roots `1−a` and 0) and any `λ ∈ [0.75, 1]` reaches the delay-free optimum at `τ = 4`; under-estimating is safe (`λ = 0.5` is 1.5× slower than delay-free, still 4× better than naive), over-estimating tightens the cap (1.54, 1.09, 0.70 at `λ = 1.25, 1.5, 2`); with `λ = 1` overlap gives 2–17× shorter wallclock at `C = 64, 1024`. Stylised: noiseless quadratic, shared Hessian, exact or secant-recovered curvature.

## 1. Setting
Loss `½Σa_ix_i²`; a round runs `H` inner steps from the global point `x_t`, giving pseudo-gradient `g_t = s x_t`. The blocking outer step is `x_{t+1} = x_t − αg_t`. In overlapped mode `g_t` only reaches the server `τ` rounds later, so `x_{t+1} = x_t − αg_{t−τ}`. A round then costs `max(H, (H+C)/(τ+1))`: `τ+1` rounds share one round trip of `H+C`. With `η = 1/a_max`, the top mode has `s = 1`, and the flat mode `s_min ≈ κ_H^{-1}`.

## 2. Exact stability and rates
For mode `a = αs` the recursion `x_{t+1} = x_t − a x_{t−τ}` is the classical delayed-gradient equation. Its roots solve `z^{τ+1} − z^τ + a = 0`; it is stable iff `a < 2 sin(π/(2(2τ+1)))` (2, 1, 0.618, 0.445, 0.347 for `τ = 0..4`; `≈ π/(2τ+1)` asymptotically). Numerically the spectral radius crosses 1 within 0.1% of this value for `τ ∈ {0,…,4,8,16,32}` (E1). The radius is minimised at the double root `z = τ/(τ+1)` with `a* = τ^τ/(τ+1)^{τ+1}` (radius 0.5, 0.667, 0.75, 0.833, 0.909 for `τ = 1,2,3,5,10`), so even a single perfectly-conditioned mode needs `ln(1/ε)/ln((τ+1)/τ) ≈ τ ln(1/ε)` rounds (E2). For a spectrum `[s_min, s_max]` the tuned `α` (golden section on the worst endpoint) and the exact rate follow from the roots; a literal simulation with inner loops matches to ≤ 0.001 in the rate (E7: 0.9969/0.9970, 0.9851/0.9852, 0.8463/0.8456).
**Plain averaging breaks** (E5): `α = 1`, `s = 1` gives `a = 1`, which is on the boundary at `τ = 1` (radius 1.0000) and beyond it for `τ = 2, 3, 6` (radius 1.151, 1.184, 1.175; `10^17–10^21` blow-up in 300 rounds). Overlap therefore needs an outer learning rate below `2 sin(π/(4τ+2))/s_max`, i.e. under-relaxation.

## 3. No free lunch
Blocking sync tunes to `α ≈ 2/(s_min+s_max)`, rate `≈ 1 − 2s_min`. With delay the cap on `α` cuts the slow mode's progress per round by `≈ 2 sin(π/(4τ+2))/2`, so rounds grow by `≈ 2/(2 sin(π/(4τ+2))) ≈ (2τ+1)/π` (for large `τ`), while the period falls by at most `τ+1`. The product `(τ+1)·2 sin(π/(4τ+2))` is 2 at `τ = 0, 1` (break-even at `τ = 1`: half the period, twice the rounds), 1.854, 1.780, 1.736 at `τ = 2, 3, 4`, and tends to `π/2`, so the best overlap ever is `2/(π/2) = 1.27×` *worse* in the sync-dominated limit. Numbers (E3, `κ = 1000`, `H = 16`, `C ≫ H`, rounds to 10⁻⁶): 435 rounds blocking; 877, 1421, 1974, 2530, 4762, 9234 at `τ = 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16`, giving time ratios 1.008, 1.089, 1.134, 1.163, 1.216, 1.249. E4 confirms with a grid over `H ∈ {1,4,16,64}` (capped by a drift/bias budget as in `local-sgd-bias`) and `τ ∈ {0,…,16}`: the optimum is always blocking (`τ = 0`) for `C ∈ {64, 1024}` and `H_max ∈ {16, 64}` — the free `H` knob (which saturates `κ_H → 1`) is a better way to amortise sync than staleness.

## 4. Delay compensation
On a quadratic the server can predict the fresh pseudo-gradient: `g_t = s x_t = g_{t−τ} + s(x_t − x_{t−τ})`, and `x_t − x_{t−τ}` is a sum of its own applied updates. The curvature `s` is recovered per coordinate by a secant of two past pseudo-gradients (exact on a quadratic). With estimate ratio `λ`: `x_{t+1} = x_t − α(g_{t−τ} + λ s(x_t − x_{t−τ}))`, characteristic `z^{τ+1} − (1−λa)z^τ + (1−λ)a`. At `λ = 1` the roots are `1−a` and `0` (multiplicity `τ`), so delay disappears from the rate: rate 0.9687 at `κ = 1000`, `H = 16` for every `τ`. E6 (`τ = 4`): `λ = 0.75, 1.0` reach the delay-free 435 rounds; `λ = 0.5` needs 631 (1.5×), `λ = 0.25` 1630 (3.7×), naive `λ = 0` 2530 (5.8×); over-estimates cost `λ = 1.25, 1.5, 2`: 598, 849, 1325 rounds with stability caps 1.54, 1.09, 0.70. Under-estimating the curvature is thus the safe side (any `λ ∈ [0.5, 1]` keeps the delay-free cap `a < 2`), over-estimating is not. Wallclock (E4, `κ = 1000`, `C ∈ {64, 1024}`): 5.0× and 17.0× faster than blocking at `H_max = 16` (`τ` up to 16), 2.1× and 17.0× at `H_max = 64`; latency is fully hidden once `τ ≥ C/H`.

## 5. Limits and next steps
The compensation needs a good per-coordinate curvature estimate; with stochastic gradients the secant is noisy, the extrapolation `λ s (x_t − x_{t−τ})` amplifies noise by `≈ λ s`, and heterogeneous workers (`local-sgd-bias`) make `x_t − x_{t−τ}` no longer the only drift. Momentum on the outer step (`outer-momentum`) shrinks the stable region further and should be analysed jointly. Real overlap also applies partial (per-fragment) staleness, which shrinks the effective `τ` per parameter. Related: `outer-momentum`, `local-sgd-bias`, `delayed-routing`, `stale-rollouts`.
