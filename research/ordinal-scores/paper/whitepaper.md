# Ordinal proper scores for binned drift reports: the ranked probability score

*Duncan Haywood. MIT licence. Code and experiments: `../src`, `../tests`, `../experiments`.*

## Abstract
`categorical-scores` compared Brier, spherical and log on an unordered K-simplex. Verifier reports about drift magnitude are ordered: being one bin off is benign, being nine bins off is not. Brier, spherical and log are all permutation-invariant and cannot see that. We study the ranked probability score (RPS) `Σ_{k<K−1}(R_k−1[y≤k])²`, `R` the report CDF, range `K−1`. Exact results: regret `Σ_k(R_k−P_k)²`; RPS equals the sum of binary Brier scores of the events `{y≤k}`; worst-case regret is attained at a vertex; the local incentive to move mass between bins `d` apart is exactly `d·ε²` versus Brier's flat `2ε²`, i.e. `d/(K−1)` of Brier's incentive per unit of payment range; a report translated by `s` bins costs RPS `s`-linearly while Brier saturates at overlap; and putting weight on one threshold `t` gives a decision-regret transfer `≤ 2√(excess weighted RPS)`, asymptotically sharp. Standard proper-scoring facts (RPS is the discretised CRPS) applied to a verifier-pricing question; not new theory.

## 1. Setup
Outcome `y∈{0..K−1}`, truth `P`, report `R` (CDFs; the last CDF value is 1 for any report). Expected RPS regret of reporting `r` against `p` is exactly `Σ_k (R_k−P_k)²`, because each threshold term is a binary Brier score with mean `P_k` (tests: matches enumeration to 1e-12, also with per-threshold weights `w_k≥0`). Hence RPS is strictly proper when all `w_k>0`, and `RPS = Σ_k` binary Brier of `{y≤k}` (test, 1e-12). Its range is `K−1`: an unweighted RPS pays more for the same relative error as `K` grows, so payments should be normalised by range when compared.

## 2. Distance-proportional incentive
For a perturbation `δ` with `Σδ=0` the regret is `Σ_k(cumsum δ)_k²`. Moving `ε` mass between bins `d` apart gives `dε²` (RPS) against `2ε²` (Brier) for every `d≥1`, so per unit of range the ratio RPS/Brier is exactly `d/(K−1)` (E1, K=10: 0.111, 0.222, 0.556, 1.000 for d=1,2,5,9). RPS deliberately gives little incentive to resolve near-miss errors and full incentive to resolve gross ones; Brier spends the same incentive on a one-bin error as on a nine-bin error. Whether that is desirable depends on whether tolerance is set in bins: if a one-bin error is inside the tolerance, RPS is the right shape.

## 3. Worst-case regret and translated misreports
The regret is convex in `r`, so its maximum over the simplex is at a vertex: `max_j Σ_k (P_k−1[j≤k])²` (random search never exceeds it; the vertex attains it). E2 (K=10): uniform truth 2.85 of range 9 (Brier 0.90 of 2), a truth at the top bin 8.91 of 9 (Brier 1.98 of 2). A report translated by `s` bins (narrow truth on two bins) costs RPS 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5 for s=1..4 but Brier 0.5, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0: once supports are disjoint Brier cannot tell a near misreport from a far one (E3). Smoothing a bell truth with neighbour blur is charged 1.5× Brier at these widths and about `1/5` of the log KL (E4), so RPS does not over-punish harmless smoothing relative to log.

## 4. Pricing one tolerance decision
Let the action be accept iff `P(y≤t)≥½`, with symmetric unit costs, so acting on `r` instead of `p` has regret `|2P_t−1|` when the decisions differ. They differ only if `|R_t−P_t|≥|P_t−½|`, so regret `≤2|R_t−P_t|`; with weight on threshold `t` only, excess weighted RPS is `(R_t−P_t)²`, giving decision regret `≤ 2√(excess)`. Over 20 000 random pairs (6 341 with a wrong decision) the ratio regret/bound reaches 1.000, so the constant 2 is sharp (as `R_t↓½`, `P_t↓½` from the other side). This is the ordinal counterpart of the calibration function in `decision-regret-transfer`: a weighted RPS lets a pool pay for exactly the threshold its slashing rule uses, and an unweighted RPS pays for all thresholds at once with the transfer holding threshold by threshold, at a loss `1/w_t` in the constant.

## 5. Limits
Risk-neutral verifiers; one report per task; exact truth distribution known to the analysis; bin edges are fixed and assumed correct (bin choice itself changes the information the report carries). Not tested: RPS with continuous outcomes (the histogram case is the exact discretisation), or against a noisy referee (`noisy-referee`).
