# Sag twin: an energy-bucket battery twin overstates constant-power flight time by a power- and resistance-dependent factor, and a single calibrated efficiency does not fix it

Pure Python, no dependencies. Real system: a pack whose open-circuit voltage falls linearly with charge drawn (6S, 25.2 → 18.0 V over 5 Ah, 108 Wh), series resistance `R`, a constant-power load `P`, and a 19.8 V terminal cutoff. The twin is an ideal source (`R = 0`): all stored energy reaches the load and runtime is `E/P`. Results (all from `experiments/results.txt`; the closed form is checked against numerical integration of `dt = dx/I` and an energy balance in the tests): (1) the real runtime has the closed form `t = [∫(v + √(v²−4RP)) dv] / (2Pm)` from the cutoff voltage `v_c = V_c + RP/V_c` to `V0`; (2) the fraction of the twin's energy that is delivered falls with both `P` and `R`: at 150 W it is 0.954, 0.909, 0.818, 0.641 for `R` = 0.03, 0.06, 0.12, 0.24 Ω, and at `R = 0.12` it falls from 0.939 at 50 W to 0.358 at 550 W; (3) a twin calibrated to one efficiency at 150 W is exact there but 12.9% pessimistic at 50 W and +17%, +40%, +74% optimistic at 250, 350, 450 W; (4) a flight exists only below `P_start = V_c(V0−V_c)/R` (445 W at 0.24 Ω, 891 W at 0.12 Ω), a limit the energy twin does not have; (5) sizing payload for a 20-minute hover (150 W at 1 kg, power ∝ mass^1.5) the twin allows 0.417 kg, the real pack 0.349, 0.288, 0.187, 0.034 kg at `R` = 0.03…0.24 Ω, i.e. the twin overstates payload by 20%, 45%, 124%, 1124%; (6) for a fleet with lognormal `R` (median 0.12 Ω, σ = 0.35) the twin's payload gives a median 13.9 min and a 5th-percentile 9.4 min flight, and sizing for 5% of packs falling short cuts payload by 85% relative to the twin.

```bash
cd research/sag-twin
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v   # 10 tests, under 1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python3 experiments/run.py                 # a few seconds; output in experiments/results.txt
```

**Builds on.** The UAV and field-robot simulation-fidelity direction of RECUV (<https://www.colorado.edu/recuv/>) and the digital-twin / sim-to-real direction of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>); no specific paper from those groups is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by them. The Thevenin battery model is classical (Chen and Rincón-Mon 2006; Plett 2015) and hover power scaling is momentum theory (Leishman 2006). Companion to `uav-energy-twin` and `roundtrip-twin` (speed- and wind-dependent energy there; here, the battery's internal loss and cutoff).

Stylised: simulated "real" system, no battery data; linear open-circuit curve, constant series resistance (real resistance depends on state of charge, temperature and current; the lognormal fleet in result 6 is an assumption, not a measured distribution), no RC transient, no capacity fade or rate-capacity (Peukert) effect, no thermal feedback from `I²R` heating, perfect constant-power load, and hover power taken as `P0 (m/m0)^{3/2}` without motor or propeller efficiency changes. The twin is a deliberately naive baseline; a twin with a fitted resistance would reproduce the results by construction. MIT.
