# Loop closure tuned in a twin: what independent odometry noise does to a SLAM chain's claimed map error

Pure Python, no dependencies. A robot integrates `n` odometry increments, re-observes its start-relative pose (a loop closure) and spreads the residual over the chain, `x̂_k = Σ_{i≤k} u_i + g k ρ`. For any real increment-error covariance the error variance at pose `k` is exact, `v_k − 2 g k a_k + g²k²W` (`v_k = Var S_k`, `a_k = Cov(S_k,S_n)`, `W = Var ρ`), with per-pose optimum share `a_k/W`. A twin with independent increments (variance `σ²`) picks `g_t = σ²/(nσ²+σ_c²)` and claims `kσ² − k²σ⁴/(nσ²+σ_c²)`. Base case (chosen, not measured): `n = 40`, odometry sd 1 cm, closure sd 1 cm; real errors add a bias random walk of per-step variance `q`. Results, all from `experiments/results.txt`: (i) a *constant* shared drift is nearly absorbed by the linear spread (drift sd 0.004: worst-pose sd 0.03207 vs the twin's claimed 0.03201; only at 0.02 does it show, 0.0337) — a negative result for the intuitive "correlated drift" story; (ii) a *drifting* bias is not: the real worst-pose sd is 1.06 / 1.49 / 2.42 / 3.62 × the claim for `q` = 1e-7 / 1e-6 / 4e-6 / 1e-5, while the twin's gain barely changes (`g n` 0.976 vs 0.984–1.000 for the end-matching gain, which does *not* help: worst-pose sd 0.0484 vs 0.0476 at `q=1e-6`) — the twin's error is in what it *claims*, not in the gain it picks; the best per-pose shares (needs the real covariance) recover only 12–25% for `q ≥ 1e-6` (0.0419 vs 0.0476); (iii) a mission rule "close loops at least every `n` steps so the claimed worst-pose sd ≤ 4 cm" certifies `n = 62` for every `q`, while the true limit is 51 / 33 / 23 (real sd at 62 steps: 4.5 / 7.9 / 14.3 cm); (iv) an audit — mean of `m` squared errors at pose 20 over the claim, one-sided at 5% — has power 0.19 / 0.32 / 0.60 / 0.92 / 1.00 at `m` = 1 / 3 / 10 / 30 / 100 when the true ratio is 2.21 (`q=1e-6`), but only 0.06–0.22 at ratio 1.12. Closed forms are checked against 40–60k-run Monte Carlo (within 1–2%). See `paper/whitepaper.md`.

**Builds on.** The SLAM, multi-robot mapping and sim-to-real-for-perception directions of ARPG (<https://arpg.colorado.edu/>); no specific paper from that lab is reproduced and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by it. Loop closure as linear-Gaussian least squares on a pose chain is textbook (Thrun, Burgard & Fox 2005, *Probabilistic Robotics*, MIT Press; Cadena et al. 2016, *IEEE T-RO*, arXiv:1606.05830 for the SLAM survey); consistency of a filter's claimed covariance is assessed by NEES (Bar-Shalom, Li & Kirubarajan 2001, *Estimation with Applications to Tracking and Navigation*, Wiley). Companion to `twin-audit`, `occupancy-twin`, `latency-twin`, `twin-transfer`.

Stylised: 1-D chain, a single loop closure of known variance, linear spreading of the residual (not a full pose-graph optimiser, no rotation, no data association), Gaussian noise, one drift model (bias random walk) and chosen parameters, no real odometry data. MIT.
