# Detection loss from a Gaussian-clutter twin: Pd for CA- and OS-CFAR by quadrature, and an SNR shortfall that no threshold repair removes

> **AI disclosure.** Produced with AI assistance: the model, code, experiments and text were generated by Claude (Anthropic) through Claude Code, working from a research brief by Duncan Haywood. Every number is produced by `experiments/run.py`; the references were checked by an AI agent and may still contain errors.

*Stylised: square-law detection, `N=16` reference cells, Swerling-I target of constant local SNR that shares the test cell's texture, unit-mean gamma texture of shape ν independent per reference cell (K-distributed power; independence across cells is a physically unrealistic worst case, see Model), thresholds set in a Gaussian-clutter twin. Pure Python; every number is from `experiments/results.txt`. The laws are one-dimensional integrals evaluated by numerical quadrature; each is checked against direct simulation (`tests/`, results section 2) and its numerical error is measured (results section 7).*

## Question
`radar-clutter-twin` showed that a CA-CFAR threshold set in a Gaussian twin inflates the real false-alarm probability (Pfa), and that raising the threshold repairs it. It left open (i) whether a different detector, OS-CFAR, is less exposed (also asked, with an additive target and by Monte Carlo, in `cfar-family-twin`; see Related projects), and (ii) what the repair costs in *detection*: how much stronger a target must be to reach the same detection probability (Pd) once the threshold is honest. That second number is what a twin should be promising when it is used to size a sensor or a mission.

## Model
Cell power is `τ·e`, `e~Exp(1)` speckle, `τ~Gamma(ν,1/ν)` texture (ν→∞ is the twin): the compound K-distribution model of sea clutter (Ward 1981; Ward, Tough & Watts 2006). In that model the texture is the slowly varying local power and is normally correlated over many range cells, while speckle decorrelates from cell to cell. Drawing `τ` independently for every reference cell, as here, is therefore physically unrealistic: it is the extreme of zero texture correlation, a worst case for any window estimate of the local level. With fully correlated texture (one `τ` per window) both detectors are exactly CFAR and the effect disappears (result 6); real clutter lies in between, so the inflation and shortfall numbers below are upper-end illustrations, not predictions for a radar. A target of local SNR `s` makes the test cell `τ0(1+s)e0`. CA-CFAR (Finn & Johnson 1968) compares it with `α·mean(reference)`, OS-CFAR (Rohling 1983) with `α·` the `k`-th smallest reference cell (`k=8,12,14` of 16). Scaling the test cell by `1+s` is the same as dividing `α` by `1+s`, so Pd(α,s)=Pfa(α/(1+s)) in every world, twin or real.
- Twin: CA `Pfa=(1+α/N)^{-N}`; OS `Pfa=Π_{i<k}(N−i)/(N−i+α)` (Rohling 1983). These are closed forms.
- Real, CA: the one-dimensional integral over the test-cell texture of `radar-clutter-twin`, evaluated by quadrature.
- Real, OS: reference cells are iid with marginal survival `S(z)=E exp(−z/τ)`, `F=1−S`, so `F(Z_(k))~Beta(k,N−k+1)` and `Pfa=∫ S(αz)·kC(N,k)F^{k−1}(1−F)^{N−k} dF`. The integral representation is exact for this model, but the numbers are *numerical quadrature*, not a closed form: the texture expectation is a trapezoid rule on a log grid, `S` is tabulated and interpolated on a log-`z` grid, and the `dF` integral is a midpoint rule on that grid; result 7 gives the accuracy as measured. Conditioning on the `k`-th order statistic is the standard route to OS-CFAR performance (Rohling 1983), and CA/OS-CFAR analysis in non-Gaussian and K-distributed clutter has a long literature (Watts 1985; Gandhi & Kassam 1988; Armstrong & Griffiths 1991); the integral is not claimed as new.

## Results
1. **Quadrature laws vs simulation.** Against 400k-trial simulation at design 1e-2 (simulation first): OS k=12 Pfa 0.0361 vs quadrature 0.0364 (ν=2), 0.0214 vs 0.0215 (ν=5); Pd at 9 dB SNR, CA 0.4752 vs 0.4743 (ν=2), 0.5193 vs 0.5194 (ν=5), OS 0.4889 vs 0.4877, 0.5149 vs 0.5159. The differences are of the size of the Monte Carlo standard error (0.8–1.1% for Pfa, 0.15–0.17% for Pd), so this check bounds the quadrature error only at that level; result 7 measures it directly.
2. **OS-CFAR is not immune.** Real/design Pfa at design 1e-4, twin thresholds: ν=2: CA 30×, OS k=8/12/14 42×/35×/26×; ν=5: 9.2×, 8.5×/9.2×/8.1×; ν=20: 2.4×, 2.0×/2.3×/2.2×. These inflations replicate `cfar-family-twin` (30.1× and 34.9× there for CA and its OS75, the same as CA and OS k=12 here) by an independent numerical route. Order statistics do not fix a marginal-shape error: the ordering of detectors varies with rank, ν and design Pfa (at 1e-6, OS k=8 is better than CA: 322× vs 358× at ν=2). The threshold repair costs about the same as for CA: 3.7 dB (CA) and 3.7–4.6 dB (OS) at ν=2, 1.9–2.1 dB at ν=5, 0.6 dB at ν=20.
3. **Detection loss is real and larger than the threshold repair.** SNR for Pd=0.9 at Pfa 1e-4 (dB): the twin promises 20.7 (CA); real clutter with an honest threshold needs 27.0 (ν=2), 23.5 (ν=5), 21.5 (ν=20), a shortfall of 6.3, 2.8, 0.8 dB. The shortfall exceeds the threshold correction alone (3.7, 1.9, 0.6 dB) because the real Pd curve is also shallower than the twin's, so the ν=2 target must be about 6 dB above what the twin said. OS k=12 has shortfall 6.1, 2.7, 0.8 dB: the same story.
4. **Uncalibrated, the twin's detector is wrong on both axes.** At the twin's threshold and the twin's promised 20.7 dB target, real Pd is 0.84 (ν=2) and 0.88 (ν=5), not 0.90, while real Pfa is 30× and 9× the design. False alarms are inflated and detections are missing at the same time.
5. **No ranking reversal here.** In the twin the SNR ordering is CA < OS k=14 (+0.50 dB) < OS k=12 (+0.62) < OS k=8 (+1.48). In real clutter CA stays best at every ν tested down to 1.5; the OS penalty only shrinks (k=12: +0.62 dB in the twin, +0.43 at ν=2, +0.33 at ν=1.5). A twin would have picked the right detector in this model; a reversal, if one exists, needs a different clutter model (e.g. outliers in the reference window, which this model excludes by construction) — this is a negative result on the detector-selection question, and the same conclusion `cfar-family-twin` reaches (CA best after repair) with an additive target independent of texture.
6. **Spatial correlation removes the problem.** With one texture draw per window (fully correlated texture), Pfa is on design for both CA (0.0101, 0.0102) and OS (0.0102, 0.0103) at design 0.0100 (simulation, ν=2 and 5). Results 2–5 therefore rest entirely on the assumption of independent texture per reference cell, which is unphysical for clutter whose texture is correlated over many cells; intermediate correlation lengths, where CFAR loss in K-distributed clutter has been analysed before (Armstrong & Griffiths 1991), are not modelled here.
7. **Quadrature accuracy, as measured.** With the texture fixed at 1, the same quadrature reproduces the closed-form twin Pfa for CA and OS (k=8/12/14) at design 1e-2, 1e-4 and 1e-6 within a relative 4.0e-04; doubling every grid resolution at ν=2 changes Pfa and Pd by at most 1.6e-04 relative. The numerical error is far below the Monte Carlo resolution of result 1. It has not been compared with a closed-form K-distribution survival function.

## Related projects
`cfar-family-twin` asks the same question with the same clutter model and the same Gaussian twin, and much of results 2 and 5 repeats it: the Pfa inflation at design 1e-4, ν=2 (CA 30×, OS k=12 35× here; 30.1× and 34.9× there), the cost of the threshold repair (3.70 dB for CA and 4.07 dB for OS k=12 in both), and the conclusion that CA detects best once thresholds are repaired. Those are replications by an independent numerical route (quadrature here, Rao-Blackwellised Monte Carlo there), not new findings, and `cfar-family-twin` also covers a log-t detector and a known-scale oracle that attributes the inflation to the test cell's own texture, which this project does not. What this project adds: (a) Pfa and Pd for OS-CFAR in iid-textured clutter by deterministic quadrature with measured numerical error (result 7), which reaches design 1e-6 without rare-event simulation; (b) a different target, a Swerling-I return that shares the test cell's texture, for which `Pd(α,s)=Pfa(α/(1+s))` (`cfar-family-twin` adds an independent Rayleigh target); (c) the detection cost as an SNR shortfall for Pd 0.9 at Pfa 1e-4 (6.3/2.8/0.8 dB for CA at ν=2/5/20), which exceeds the threshold repair, rather than Pd at fixed SNR; (d) the uncalibrated operating point, where Pd is short and Pfa inflated at once (result 4). Both projects extend `radar-clutter-twin` and share its independent-texture assumption.

## Limitations
Not evidence about any real radar: no measured data, iid per-cell texture (physically unrealistic, and fully correlated texture removes the effect, result 6), a target that shares the test cell's texture and has constant local SNR (a target whose return is independent of clutter texture would give a different Pd law), single-pulse detection, no interfering targets in the reference window (where OS-CFAR is normally preferred), one window length, and Pd/Pfa laws for the two detectors only. The grid quadrature is checked against simulation, against the twin closed forms with the texture fixed, and by grid refinement (result 7), but not against a closed-form K-distribution survival function. Detection loss is reported at a single operating point (Pd 0.9, Pfa 1e-4).

## Next steps
Interfering targets in the reference window (where OS should win, testing whether a twin that omits them mis-ranks detectors); a target return independent of texture; calibrate ν from real cells and propagate that estimation error into an SNR shortfall interval; connect to `randomized-twin` by randomizing ν in the twin.
