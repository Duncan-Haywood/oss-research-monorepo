Research portfolio

Open-source (MIT) research code and write-ups on incentive-compatible verification of decentralized ML training, building on algorithmic economics and information elicitation (CU Boulder, Frongillo, Waggoner) and Gensyn's verifiable-ML work.

Peer-prediction markets for decentralized ML training verification

Proper scoring rules, Peer Truth Serum, Correlated Agreement and LMSR aggregation, with eight follow-ups on mechanism-aware adversaries (trust farming, whitewashing, closed-loop attackers) and defences. Pure-Python, dependency-free, 72 tests.

Code, results and limitations

Markets as routers: LMSR = Hedge, quadratic potential = sparsemax

Cost-function markets as online learners for modular, continual-learning expert routing: verified equivalence and loss/regret bounds, and a switching-environment simulation where the quadratic market stays sparse and low-regret at liquidity levels where LMSR degrades. Small-scale; limitations stated.

Code, white paper and results

Wagering mechanisms as routers for modular, continually learning experts

Weighted-score wagering (Lambert et al.) as a budget-balanced, replayable mixture-of-experts gate under recurring regime drift: plain wagering locks in, fixed-share wealth tax repairs it but subsidises free-riders. Pure-Python, 13 tests.

Code, results and limitations

Property elicitation for verifiable ML training

Quantile, expectile and Bregman scoring rules, a check that variance is not elicitable alone but is jointly, and a study of pricing benign-drift thresholds for replica verification. Pure-Python, 6 tests.

Economics of refereed verification

Inspection-game equilibrium for optimistic verification: stake substitutes for re-execution, extra verifiers dilute rewards (free-riding saturates), and joint tolerance/stake design under floating-point drift. Pure-Python, 7 tests, stylised model.

Code and white paper

Bribing the verifiers

Closed-form bribe floor for buying verifier silence in refereed verification: a sharp collusion-proof stake (falls as 1/m verifiers), and planted-fault jackpots that lift the floor. Verified against brute force and Monte Carlo. Pure-Python, 7 tests, stylised model.

Code and white paper

Reproducible operators and refereed training

Measured float32 drift across reduction orders (relative drift is Cauchy-tailed, state-level drift is a few ulps), a Merkle-committed training trace with a teacher-forced referee, and the finding that a per-step tolerance is a cumulative hidden-deviation budget while bitwise-reproducible operators remove it. Pure-Python, 10 tests.

Code and white paper

The subsidy cost of privacy in prediction markets

Differentially private LMSR via the binary-tree mechanism: error scales as 1/(bε), tree noise beats per-release noise ~15×, and privacy noise is exploitable so market-maker loss far exceeds b ln 2. Pure-Python, 6 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Do verifiers and solvers learn the audit equilibrium?

Hedge dynamics in the inspection game: a conserved potential, a closed-form cycle period (matched to simulation within 0.1%), time-averages that converge while per-round audit probability collapses, and optimistic Hedge that converges last-iterate. Pure-Python, 8 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Many verifiers, one solver: the volunteer's dilemma in refereed verification

Closed-form m-verifier audit equilibrium (detection pinned at s/(s+S), redundancy bounded by ln(1/(1-q))/q, cheating rising with m under split rewards), a saddle-point stability result, Hedge dynamics that concentrate audit burden on a subset, and bandit learners that need an exploration floor. Pure-Python, 10 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Paying for improvements, not for overfitting

A model-improvement market paying holdout-loss decreases: the budget telescopes, but naive payment feedback lets a data-less adversary be paid while the published model's true loss worsens; a Ladder-style threshold removes the attack at a measured cost to honest small contributors. Pure-Python, 4 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Are stake and audits substitutes in trace verification?

Sampled audits of a committed training trace under capped slashing: cheating payoff is convex in the number of corrupted steps, and deterrence holds iff min(F, f·k) ≥ T·g. Stake and audits are complements, so stake must scale with job length. Exact hypergeometric computation, 7 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

How many bits does a probability report need?

Regret of reporting from a finite grid under Brier and log scores: a high-resolution law for the optimal grid (matched to 0.3%), a ln N loss for uniform grids under log score, and a 13× penalty for raw minifloat formats versus symmetrised ones. Pure-Python, 7 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

What does it cost to pay a verifier to check?

Effort elicitation with proper scoring rules in an exact beta-binomial model: implementable efforts are the concave envelope of the value curve, Brier information rent is exactly (n−1)/(a+b) times effort cost, and the log score needs half Brier's payout spread on rare-fault priors. Pure-Python, 6 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

How much should a router extremize?

Pooling calibrated experts with shared training data: the Bayes-optimal logit exponent a* = n/(1+(n−1)ρ) (matched to a numerical argmin), extra experts saturating at 1/ρ effective experts, naive independence pooling doing worse than chance at ρ=0.9, and an online learner that finds a*. Pure-Python, 6 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

What does a proper score buy when effort is private?

Scoring-rule payments as moral-hazard contracts for costly inference: a closed-form shirking threshold (matched to 1%), first-best effort at scale equal to marginal value with a fee, and under limited liability only 48–65% of first-best surplus, with log score cheaper than Brier at high effort. Pure-Python, 6 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

How do you pay for effort when nobody knows the truth?

Peer prediction for unverifiable labelling and judging work: output agreement pays 1.0 for always answering the majority label versus 0.68 for honesty, the Dasgupta–Ghosh payoff is exactly 2p(1−p)gigj, effort becomes a coordination game with a closed-form threshold (12.8× higher for rare-positive tasks), and a cost-optimal gold-check rate √(c·gH/G) removes the shirking equilibrium. Pure-Python, 8 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

What does a verifier's score gap cost in decisions?

Exact calibration functions turning a proper-score gap into a certified cap on accept/audit/slash regret: Brier transfers as ε², log as a KL, polyhedral hinge linearly but without proper elicitation, and an audit action tightens the guarantee; the certificate is never violated in simulation and is attained by a two-point verifier. Pure-Python, 7 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Which surrogate should train a verifier that can escalate?

Reject-option surrogates for accept/reject/escalate decisions: the generalised-hinge conditional risk embeds the Bayes action at its kinks, regret transfers linearly with constant exactly 2d (grid-verified), logistic transfer degrades as 2d(1−d)/δ, and a negative result shows logistic-plus-thresholds still learns faster in scalar SGD. Pure-Python, 6 tests, stylised.

Code and white paper

Are reward pools identity-proof?

Sybil splitting and whale merging under stake-weighted pools: only linear-in-stake weights are split-invariant, per-head pools are captured at k* = √(Rn/c) − n identities (exact match), a sharp per-identity deterrence fee, exponents above 1 reward merging, and weighted-score wagering is split-neutral. Pure-Python, 7 tests, stylised.
